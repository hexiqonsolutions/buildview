# Content strategy

30 days of execution are in `08-30-day-content-calendar.md`. This file is the system that calendar belongs to. Nothing here is scheduled or published.

## Commercial aim

Each post should help one of these people recognise their own project and take the next step toward a demo:

- Developer or project owner who cannot see several sites without travelling.
- Architect who is reviewing execution from the office.
- Contractor who is tired of rebuilding the same progress update.
- PMC or consultant who needs a record of milestones and issues.

Posts that entertain a general construction audience, or that look like an interior offer, are out of scope even if they would gain followers.

## Rules for every asset

- Show only capabilities classified as safe in `01-product-positioning.md`.
- Use the live product’s words: virtual tours, compare progress, progress reports, document hub, issue tracking, project timeline, client dashboard.
- If a screen recording needs a real project, use one you have permission to show, or a demo project, and do not imply it is a public case study.
- Do not name a client, a tower, a contract value, or a result.
- Do not show someone’s face, phone number, drawing, or site unless you have the right to publish it.
- The interior creative on @buildview.360 is not a template.
- One idea per post. One primary CTA.
- Write the alt text before you post. The calendar includes it.
- Design from `09-branding-and-visual-guidelines.md`. Poppins for titles, Open Sans for body, colours from the product.

## Pillars

### 1. Product demonstrations

What: a short look at the real interface. A tour opening, a compare, a document list, an issue, a timeline.

Why: buyers in this category have seen slogans. A recognisable product screen is what makes the account look like a software company.

Cadence: at least one Reel or carousel a week. Never a tour of five modules in 15 seconds.

### 2. Construction technology education

What: a plain explanation of a practice, such as why a dated visual record is more useful than a loose photo folder, or what a stakeholder can review remotely.

Why: people who are not searching for “BuildView” still search for the problem. Education earns the profile visit. The last line can mention the product without turning the post into a brochure.

Cadence: one post a week. No lectures, no invented research.

### 3. 360° virtual walkthroughs

What: how a Matterport tour sits inside a BuildView project, and what a remote review is for.

Why: this is the distinctive visual. It is also the easiest post to misunderstand as a photography service, so the caption must say it is part of project monitoring.

Cadence: about one in four feed posts. Always pair the tour with a job to be done: review progress, compare a date, brief a stakeholder.

### 4. Construction progress documentation

What: reports, timelines, site images, and the idea of a history the team can return to.

Why: many buyers do not want a headset tour. They want an update they can trust. This pillar speaks to them without claiming the unfinished pre-slab checklist.

Cadence: one post a week, alternating with tours so the grid is not only video.

### 5. Builder and developer pain points

What: the cost of scattered updates — photos in a chat, drawings in email, a report rebuilt before every meeting. Describe the pattern. Do not invent a story about a named company.

Why: the pain is why someone books a demo. The post should sound like a site meeting, not a complaint about “the industry”.

Cadence: one post a week in the first month, then reduce if the grid starts to feel negative. Follow every pain post with a product post.

### 6. Client and project collaboration

What: roles, comments, who can raise an issue, who can change its status, and why a read-only stakeholder still has a place.

Why: the sale often includes more than one company. Showing that the portal can hold them, without claiming a chat app, answers a real objection.

Cadence: two posts in the first month, plus the FAQ highlight.

### 7. Product features

What: one module, accurately. Documents mean drawings, BOQs, contracts, approvals, and technical files. Issues mean observations with an owner and a status. Compare means two visits side by side.

Why: feature posts become the highlight reels and the answers your team pastes when someone asks “what do you actually do?”.

Cadence: the first nine grid posts are mostly this pillar, on purpose.

### 8. Real-world use cases

What: the scenarios already on the public projects page — commercial towers, residential developments, industrial facilities, infrastructure, hospitality, specialised facilities. The page itself says these are scenarios, not client stories.

Why: a buyer needs to see their building type. A scenario does that without fabricating a project.

Cadence: one scenario a week after the launch grid. Say “for a commercial tower”, not “our commercial tower client”.

### 9. Brand awareness

What: the positioning line, the capture → monitor → review → deliver sequence, who the company is, and where it is based.

Why: a new account has no memory in the market. A small number of brand posts make the grid identifiable. More than that, and the account stops selling.

Cadence: the first grid post, the audience post, and the closing post of the month. No mood imagery without a sentence of product meaning.

### 10. Demo conversion

What: what the demo covers, how to book, what to have ready (project type, who will log in, what you want to monitor).

Why: this is the point of the account. If a week has no path to `/contact`, the week was content, not marketing.

Cadence: a direct demo post at the end of the launch grid, a story CTA every week, and the primary line in most captions.

## Weekly rhythm

Post feed content on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday at about 09:00 IST. That is a hypothesis: people in this market often look at their phone before they leave for site. It is not a measured best time. Move it if insights later show otherwise.

Use Tuesday, Thursday, and one weekend slot for stories. Stories carry the conversation and the sticker link. They do not have to look like ads.

Do not post seven feed units a week. Three finished posts and three stories will look more like a software company than a daily filler habit.

The 30-day calendar still specifies every day, so the team knows what the off-days are for. A story day is a planned touch, not a leftover.

## Formats

| Format | Spec | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Carousel | 1080 × 1350, 5 to 7 frames, title in the centre square | Explain one module so it can be saved |
| Reel | 1080 × 1920, 20 to 35 seconds, captions burned in | Show motion: a tour, a compare, a click path |
| Static | 1080 × 1350 | One sentence that can sit in the grid |
| Stories | 1080 × 1920, 3 to 5 frames | A question, a FAQ, or a demo reminder |

Reel covers must be readable as a square, because the grid crops them. Put the title in the middle, not at the top.

## Caption structure

1. First line: the point, with one real keyword, before the fold.
2. Three to six short lines of explanation. No jargon that the product does not use.
3. Who it is for, when that is not obvious.
4. CTA: Book a demo, plus the matching URL from `06-cta-and-conversion-strategy.md`.
5. Five hashtags from one set in `04-instagram-seo.md`.

Do not open with “Excited to announce” or a question that could belong to any brand (“Tag a site engineer”).

## What the first month must leave behind

By 8 December 2026 a stranger opening the profile should be able to answer, without watching every Reel:

- This is construction monitoring software.
- It includes 360° tours, progress records, documents, and issues.
- It is for project teams, not home-interior shoppers.
- The way in is a demo.

The nine-post grid in the branding file is that answer. The rest of the month repeats it in more depth. Do not insert a festive graphic, a hiring post, or a repost of the interior creative into that grid.

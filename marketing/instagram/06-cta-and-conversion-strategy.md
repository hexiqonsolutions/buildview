# CTA and conversion strategy

The commercial job of Instagram is a qualified demo request from a builder, developer, architect, contractor, PMC, or project owner. Reach, saves, and follows are useful only when they move someone into that conversation.

## Calls to action

**Primary — Book a demo.**

Use this on the profile button path, the last line of most captions, the ninth grid post, and every highlight that is about a decision. Destination: the contact page, via `/links` when the person is coming from the profile.

**Secondary, in this order:**

| CTA | Destination | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Explore BuildView | `/services` | They do not yet know the modules |
| See how it works | `/#platform` or the How It Works highlight | They understand the category and want the sequence |
| Request a consultation | `/contact` with interest left for them to choose | A PMC or developer with several projects, where “demo” may sound too small. The form still records it |
| Contact our team | `/contact` or the email button | They asked a specific question |

Do not add “Get a quote”, “DM for price”, or “Link in bio” as the only line. Price is not published, and “link in bio” does not say what they will get. If you need the phrase, write “Book a demo from the profile link.”

Full URLs to copy:

- Profile and general bio traffic: `https://buildview-tawny.vercel.app/links?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=buildview_profile&utm_content=bio`
- A Reel: same host and path, `utm_campaign=buildview_reels`, `utm_content` set to the reel topic, for example `virtual_tours`
- A carousel: `utm_campaign=buildview_carousel`
- A story: `utm_campaign=buildview_stories` and, when the sticker can take a URL, point it at `/contact` or `/links`
- A direct demo ask: `https://buildview-tawny.vercel.app/contact?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=buildview_profile&utm_content=book_demo`

`/links` is the right front door because it already lists Book a Demo first, then Explore BuildView, How it works, 360° tours, progress monitoring, and Contact. Someone who is not ready to book can still go deeper without leaving the brand.

## Funnel

```
Instagram post, Reel, or story
        ↓
Profile (name, bio, grid, highlights)
        ↓
/links  (what to do next)
        ↓
/services or /about  (product understanding)
        ↓
/contact  (demo form and scheduler)
        ↓
Email to the sales inbox, plus a same-day human reply
        ↓
Qualification conversation
        ↓
Live demo of the real product
        ↓
Commercial conversation. There is no public checkout.
```

Each step has one job.

| Step | Job | Failure if you skip the discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Post | One module or one problem, stated plainly | The profile gets visits from people who wanted interiors or photography |
| Profile | Confirm this is construction software and show the demo path | A good post dies on an empty bio |
| `/links` | Offer the demo without hiding the product | A direct WhatsApp link skips anyone who wants to read first |
| Services | Show tours, compare, reports, timeline, documents, issues, dashboard | They book a call expecting a camera crew only |
| Contact | Capture name, work email, company, interest, and project context | A like is not a lead |
| Reply | Human answer within one business day, which is what the form already promises | The promise on the site becomes false |
| Qualification | Role, project type, location, stage, who else decides | Demos are spent on students and homeowners |
| Demo | Screen the actual portal: a tour, a compare, a report, a document, an issue | The call repeats the Instagram captions |
| Next step | Agree what happens after the demo. Do not invent a contract in the caption | The enquiry stays “interested” |

## Landing page

**Instagram website field:** `/links`.

**Paid and “book a demo” captions:** `/contact`.

Reason: organic visitors are still deciding, so the link hub respects that. A person who tapped “Book a demo” should not pass through six buttons. The contact page is titled “Book a Construction Monitoring Demo”, includes the form, the published phone and email, the FAQ, and a scheduler.

Do not send ad traffic to the home page. The home page is a story. The contact page is the conversion.

Do not send traffic to `buildview.io`.

## Enquiry form

Use the form that is already on `/contact`. Fields that exist today:

- Full name (required)
- Work email (required)
- Company
- Interest (required): live demo, monitoring quote, enterprise deployment, virtual tour services, partnership, technical support, other
- Message (required), with the placeholder asking for project type, portfolio size, and monitoring needs
- Privacy-policy acknowledgement

That is enough for a first lead. Do not add fields to the application as part of this marketing work. If a message is thin, the follow-up in `10-lead-generation-automation.md` asks the qualification questions.

Interest “Virtual Tour Services” will attract people who want only a photography assignment. That option can stay, because the company does capture sites, but the reply should explain that capture is delivered inside the monitoring platform and ask whether they need ongoing project access. Do not build a separate “book a photographer” funnel on Instagram.

## What a qualified enquiry looks like

Keep the conversation when most of these are true:

- They are a developer, architect, contractor, PMC, consultant, or project owner. Or they work for one.
- They name a project type the product already speaks to: residential, commercial, industrial, infrastructure, hospitality, or another live construction project.
- The project is in progress or about to start, not a completed home they want photographed for sale.
- They can say who else needs to see the portal.
- They have a work email or a company name.

Close it politely when:

- They want an interior, BHK, or home-decoration quote.
- They want to buy a camera, or they want a one-off social-media reel of a site with no monitoring requirement.
- They are a student asking for a free project with no organisation behind it.
- They will not say what they build or where the project is.

## Follow-up standard

The form tells the visitor the team will respond within one business day. Instagram and WhatsApp should keep that promise.

| When | Who | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Within 10 minutes, if someone is available | Automation | Instant reply. It does not confirm a meeting |
| Same business day | A named person | Answer the question and ask the qualification questions that are still missing |
| Within 2 business days if they go quiet | Same person | One useful follow-up tied to their project type |
| Day 5 | Same person | One close-the-loop note |
| After a demo | Same person | A short recap of what was shown and the agreed next step |

Scripts are in `10-lead-generation-automation.md`. Do not add a fifth “just checking in” message.

## What to measure each week

From the website, not from follower count:

- Visits to `/links` and `/contact` with an Instagram UTM.
- Contact-form submissions, split by interest.
- How many of those named a company and a project.
- Demos held.
- Replies that were a wrong fit, so the content can be corrected.

From Instagram:

- Profile visits from the posts you actually published.
- Taps on the website button and WhatsApp, once the professional dashboard shows them.
- Reply time in the inbox.

A week with 200 profile visits and no demo requests means the bio, the grid, or the CTA is unclear. A week with three demo requests from developers or PMCs is a better result than a week with 50 new followers.
